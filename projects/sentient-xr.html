Sentient XR

Zero-install WebXR system that brings live city-scale sensor data into augmented reality.

Overview

A zero-install, city-scale WebXR augmented reality system that shows live environmental sensor data as particle fields anchored to real places, so people can see and stand inside the atmosphere without downloading an app.

The vision: make environmental data understandable and physically experienced at city scale. Instead of checking a weather app, you walk through the current state of the air, rendered at real GPS coordinates in your browser.

Experience design

  • People scan a QR code in public space and open a browser-based AR view, with an average of 7.6 seconds from scan to interaction and no install.
  • On Android, the live street view is overlaid with a particle field at the user's location.
  • Color shows temperature, from cool teal to hot red-orange. Motion speed shows wind, and turbulence shows gust intensity.

My role

  • Developed and shipped the XR, AR and WebXR builds in C#, TypeScript, Unity and Three.js, from immersive data visualization to digital twins.
  • Built the real-time pipeline that connects AWS APIs and environmental sensor networks to the AR camera view across web, iPad and Android.
  • Optimized rendering and data handling through profiling, shader work and image-processing improvements, holding 45–60 FPS.
  • Worked with researchers, designers and domain experts to turn ambiguous requirements into working prototypes, and resolved 50+ technical issues.

Technical development

01

Browser-delivered AR client

Built in Unity and exported through Needle Engine to a Three.js and WebGL runtime. With no native app wrapper, the design could not assume native-mobile performance.

02

Two modes, one codebase

A 3D exploration mode shows weather stations as particle fields over a simplified city. A camera-overlay AR mode on Android anchors one field to the user's GPS position. Both share the update loop and particle transfer functions.

03

City geometry for mobile WebGL

Low-polygon building masses, about 2,400 polygons for a focused variant and about 118,000 for the full city. A separate wireframe layer keeps edges clear, because outline shaders were not reliable in Needle's mobile WebGL export.

Challenges

Cross-platform AR

What we did

iOS Safari does not yet support WebXR AR sessions, which excludes roughly half of US smartphone users. We built a 3D fallback and named cross-platform parity as a priority for future work.

Rendering vs. accessibility

What we did

On entry-level Android hardware, frame rate dropped to 21 FPS under heavy particle load. We treated the particle budget as an accessibility decision and chose graceful degradation over excluding lower-end devices.
